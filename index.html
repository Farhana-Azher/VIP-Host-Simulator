Product case study · concept

One VIP. One view. Every brand.

How a single customer view could help hosts serve VIP players across multiple gambling brands, without losing sight of safer gambling, ownership or audit.

The problem

Hosted VIPs often hold accounts on several brands. Today, hosts piece together one relationship from many systems, which causes duplicate outreach, repeated questions for the customer and slow handoffs between teams.

Single Player View principleOne person is the anchor. Brand accounts sit beneath the same VIP record as linked child accounts. Servicing history, ownership and preferences are shown once; account-level values stay identifiable by brand.

Who it's for

Primary

VIP Host

Needs the full relationship in one place, with clear next actions and no duplicate outreach.

Secondary

Supervisor

Needs ownership, coverage gaps, onboarding and off-ramp visibility, plus control over reassignments.

Stakeholder

Compliance / Safer Gambling

Needs decisions to be explainable, restricted data to be controlled and every material action to be audited.

How we'd know it worked

Illustrative targets for the case study, to be baselined before launch.

↓ 30%

↓ 25%

↑ 20%

VIPs per host (same quality)

Capacity gain without a drop in service scores or compliance outcomes.

North-star: time from a player contacting us to a correct, compliant resolution, measured across all linked brands.

Scope at a glance

MVP foundation
  • Player 360 and linked brand accounts
  • Unified interaction timeline
  • Tasks, cases and handoff
  • Core bonus request workflow
  • Supervisor controls, permissions, audit
Post-MVP / enhanced
  • Next-best-action recommendations
  • Engagement trend and relationship risk
  • Opportunity and milestone insights
  • Recommendations support hosts; they never make high-impact decisions alone

Key dependencies

  • Golden customer identity and account-linking logic must exist before the timeline, cases and bonus workflow can sit on one record.
  • Source-of-truth per data domain, plus data-freshness rules, must be agreed before insights can be trusted.
  • Merge/split governance and auditability of identity changes need an owner before launch.

Risks & guardrails

In a gambling context, a "helpful" tool can also cause harm. These are the guardrails the product would be designed around from day one.

RiskGuardrailSeverity
Bonuses or outreach aimed at a player showing signs of harmSafer-gambling flags are checked at bonus-request time; flagged players are blocked and routed to the safer-gambling team.High
"Next best action" nudges players to spend moreRecommendations are advisory, exclude flagged players, and are reviewed with Compliance before release.High
Wrong accounts linked to one personGolden-ID matching rules, human review for low-confidence matches, and a full audit trail on merges and splits.High
Sensitive notes seen by the wrong peopleRole- and attribute-based access, sensitivity labels on notes, and no duplication per brand unless genuinely brand-specific.Medium
High-value bonuses approved without oversightValue thresholds trigger supervisor approval; every decision records its inputs.Medium
Stale or conflicting data between brandsSource-of-truth by data domain, visible "last updated" markers, and retry/idempotent integrations.Medium
Hosts over-trust a recommendationHuman-in-the-loop for any high-impact decision; the reason behind each recommendation is always visible.Low
